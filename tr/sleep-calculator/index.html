How to Calculate the Best Bedtime

Sleep is structured in 90-minute cycles, each moving through light sleep, deep sleep (slow-wave sleep), and REM sleep. Waking in the middle of a cycle — especially during deep sleep — causes sleep inertia: that groggy, disoriented feeling that can last 20–60 minutes. Waking at the end of a cycle, during light sleep, feels effortless.

The key insight: it is not just about hours slept, but where in the cycle you wake. 7.5 hours (5 complete cycles) often feels better than 8 hours if the extra 30 minutes interrupts a deep sleep cycle.

Most adults need 5–6 complete sleep cycles (7.5–9 hours). The calculator adds 14 minutes to fall-asleep time — the average sleep onset latency for most people. If you regularly fall asleep faster or slower, adjust your mental model accordingly.

Sleep Cycle Stages

  • Stage 1 (N1): Light sleep, 1–5 min — easy to wake, hypnic jerks common
  • Stage 2 (N2): True sleep, 10–25 min — body temperature drops, heart rate slows
  • Stage 3 (N3): Deep/slow-wave sleep — hardest to wake from, most restorative
  • REM sleep: Dreaming, memory consolidation — more REM in later cycles of the night

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is one sleep cycle?+
One complete sleep cycle lasts approximately 90 minutes and includes light sleep (N1, N2), deep sleep (N3), and REM sleep. A full night contains 4–6 cycles. The proportion of deep sleep is highest in early cycles; REM dominates later cycles in the night.
How many hours of sleep do adults need?+
Most adults need 7–9 hours (5–6 complete 90-minute cycles) per night. Teenagers need 8–10 hours; school-age children 9–11 hours. Chronic sleep deprivation under 6 hours is linked to increased risk of obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and cognitive decline.
Why do I feel tired after 8 hours of sleep?+
You may be waking in the middle of a sleep cycle during deep sleep, causing sleep inertia. Try adjusting your alarm by 15–30 minutes earlier or later to align with cycle boundaries. Also check for sleep apnea, which fragments sleep cycles without you knowing.
What is sleep inertia?+
Sleep inertia is the grogginess and disorientation felt immediately after waking from deep sleep. It typically lasts 15–60 minutes but can extend to 2–4 hours in severe cases. Waking during light sleep (N1/N2) or REM sleep minimizes it. Strategic naps of 20 minutes also avoid sleep inertia by staying out of deep sleep.
